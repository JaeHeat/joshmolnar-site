How to Build Confidence in Trading

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 6 min read

Most traders think confidence comes from winning. You stack a few good trades, you feel unstoppable, and then one bad day erases the whole feeling. That is not confidence. That is mood. Real trading confidence is something quieter and far more durable. It comes from trusting a process you have tested, not from the last trade on your screen. Let me explain how I built mine and how you can build yours.

Why most traders never feel confident

About 70 percent of retail traders lose money. That is not a scare tactic. It is what brokers are required to disclose by financial regulators in Europe. And most of those losses come from the same loop. You try a strategy, it loses a few times, you lose trust, you switch to something new, and the cycle repeats. You never stay with anything long enough to know if it actually works.

Confidence cannot survive that cycle. If you change your approach every time it feels uncomfortable, you are always a beginner. You never collect enough reps to separate normal losing streaks from a broken strategy.

Trading confidence starts with a tested process

The foundation is simple. You need a set of rules you have tested before you risk real money. That means backtesting your strategy across enough trades to see how it behaves in both good and bad stretches. When you know that your approach has survived hundreds of trades in historical data, a five-trade losing streak stops feeling like the end of the world. You have seen worse in the test, and the numbers still came out fine.

I am a data scientist, so I test everything with hard numbers before it touches a live account. You do not need to be a data scientist to do this. You just need to write your rules down, apply them to old charts, and count what happens. The act of testing builds trust, and trust is the raw material of confidence.

Reps at small size build trust faster than big wins

One of the biggest mistakes I see in people I mentor is trying to build confidence by trading large. The logic sounds right on the surface. If I make real money, I will feel confident. But it works the opposite way. Large positions create fear, fear leads to rule-breaking, and rule-breaking destroys whatever trust you had.

Start so small that losing does not bother you. Seriously. If a loss makes your stomach drop, your size is too big. At tiny risk, you can focus entirely on executing your plan. Every time you follow your rules, whether the trade wins or loses, you are adding a rep. After 50 or 100 clean reps, you will notice something shift. You stop hoping and start expecting, because you have seen the process work with your own hands.

Track your trades so confidence has evidence

Feelings lie. Data does not. If you are not keeping a trading journal, your confidence has no foundation. It is just a guess about how well you are doing. A simple log of every trade, your entry, your exit, whether you followed your rules, and the result, gives you something concrete to point at when doubt creeps in.

When I hit a rough patch, I open my journal and look at the last 100 trades. If I followed my rules and the numbers are where I expect them to be, the rough patch is just randomness doing what randomness does. That is not blind optimism. That is evidence. And evidence is the only thing that holds up when your emotions are telling you to quit.

Confidence and discipline feed each other

Here is something people miss. Confidence and discipline are not separate skills. They are a loop. When you follow your rules, you build confidence. When you feel confident in your process, following the rules gets easier. The hard part is getting the loop started, and the only way to start it is to follow the rules even when you do not feel like it. Especially then.

This is why I tell every person I mentor to make the first goal simple. Not profit. Not a funded account. Just follow your plan for 20 trades in a row without breaking a single rule. If you can do that, profit is a matter of time and capital. If you cannot, no strategy on earth will save you.

What destroys trading confidence

If you want to stay confident, avoid the things that tear it down.

  • Changing strategies after every loss. You never build trust in anything because you never give anything a real chance.
  • Sizing up after a win. This ties your confidence to results, which means the next loss takes it all back.
  • Comparing yourself to other traders online. You are seeing their highlights, not their losing streaks. It makes your normal results feel broken when they are not.
  • Trading without a plan. Winging it might work once or twice, but it cannot build lasting trust because there is nothing repeatable to trust.

The honest version

Confidence in trading is not a personality trait. It is not something you are born with. It is something you build, slowly, by testing a strategy, trading it at small size, tracking every result, and following your rules long enough to see the math play out. It is boring. It is supposed to be. If you want the full picture of what this kind of process looks like inside a real trading career, that is exactly what I write about on this site.

Common questions

How do you build confidence as a new trader?

Test your strategy on historical data first, then trade it at a size so small that losses do not bother you. Confidence comes from reps and evidence, not from winning streaks.

Why do I lose confidence after a losing trade?

Because your confidence is tied to results instead of process. If you trust your tested rules, a single loss is just one data point in a larger sample that you already know works.

How many trades does it take to feel confident?

There is no exact number, but most traders start feeling real trust in their process after 50 to 100 trades executed cleanly according to their plan.

Can you trade well without confidence?

Not for long. Without confidence in your process you will second-guess entries, move stops, and break rules, which turns even a good strategy into a losing one.

Does confidence mean never feeling doubt?

No. Even experienced traders feel doubt during losing stretches. The difference is that they have a journal full of evidence that their process works, so the doubt does not change their behavior.
