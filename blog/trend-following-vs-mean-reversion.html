Trend Following vs Mean Reversion: Which Fits You?

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

Almost every trading strategy you will ever see is one of two bets. Either you bet that a move keeps going, or you bet that it snaps back. That is the whole trend following vs mean reversion debate in one line. People I mentor often mix the two without knowing it, and that mix is where a lot of quiet damage comes from. So here is what each one really is, what each one feels like to trade, and how I think about choosing between them.

What trend following means

Trend following is betting that a market already moving in one direction keeps moving that way. Price is climbing, so you buy and ride along. Price is falling, so you sell and ride along. You are not guessing where the move starts or where it ends. You are trying to grab a piece of the middle.

Picture a river. A trend follower puts the boat in and goes with the current. Trades that buy a push to new highs live here, and so do trades that buy a small dip inside a bigger climb. I cover the first kind in what a breakout is.

What mean reversion means

Mean reversion sounds technical. It is a simple idea. Mean is another word for average. Reversion means going back. So mean reversion is betting that a price that has run too far from its usual level comes back toward it.

Picture a rubber band. Stretch it and it wants to snap back. A mean reversion trader waits for price to stretch far from its average, then bets on the snap. That means buying after a sharp drop and selling after a sharp jump. You are trading against the latest move, not with it.

Trend following vs mean reversion, side by side

The two styles feel completely different to live with.

  • How often you win. Trend following tends to lose more often than it wins. Many moves fizzle out and you take a small loss. Mean reversion tends to win more often, because most stretches do snap back.
  • How big the wins are. A trend winner can be large, because a real trend can run a long way. A mean reversion winner is usually small, because the trip back to average is short.
  • How it hurts. Trend following hurts slowly. You take a lot of small losses while you wait for the one move that pays. Mean reversion hurts suddenly. It works again and again, and then one day the rubber band does not snap back. It just keeps stretching.
  • When it works. Trend following needs a market that is going somewhere. Mean reversion needs a market that is bouncing back and forth between a floor and a ceiling.

Neither one is better. Winning often and winning big are two different things, and I explain why in win rate vs risk reward.

The mistake of mixing them

Here is the trap I see most. A trader buys because price is pushing higher. That is a trend bet. Then price turns and drops. Now the same trader says it has fallen too far and is due for a bounce, so they hold. That is a mean reversion excuse glued onto a trend trade.

The reason for getting in is gone, and a new reason showed up just in time to avoid taking the loss. Know which bet you are making before you click. If it is a trend trade, you get out when the trend breaks. If it is a snap back trade, you get out when the stretch runs past your stop. One trade, one idea.

Which one fits you

I would not pick by which one sounds smarter. I would pick by which kind of pain you can sit through without breaking your rules. A few honest questions help.

  • Can you lose six trades out of ten and stay calm? Trend following asks for that.
  • Can you take one large loss cleanly after a long run of small wins? Mean reversion asks for that. A stop loss is not optional here. Without one, a single runaway move can undo months of work.
  • What does your market do at the time of day you trade? Some hours tend to move in one direction. Others mostly chop sideways.

Most of my own day trading leans toward going with the move. That is a fit with my temperament. It is not proof that it is the better style. I know good traders on both sides.

If you trade a funded account, the choice matters even more. Prop firm rules cap how much you can lose in a single day. A snap back trade that keeps stretching can reach that cap very quickly, so your stop has to be set before you enter.

The bottom line

Trend following bets the move continues. Mean reversion bets it comes back. Both can work, both can fail, and most traders lose money with either one. The style is not what saves you. Small fixed risk and a rule you actually follow are what give you a chance. Pick one bet, test it on past charts, trade it small, and do not switch sides in the middle of a trade. That boring kind of process is what I mean when I talk about trading for a living.

Common questions

Is trend following better than mean reversion?

Neither is better. Trend following needs a market that is going somewhere and mean reversion needs one that is bouncing in a range. The better choice is the one whose losses you can sit through without breaking your rules.

What is mean reversion in simple terms?

It is betting that a price that has run too far from its average comes back toward it. Think of a stretched rubber band that wants to snap back.

Does mean reversion win more often than trend following?

It tends to win more often, but the wins are usually small. One move that keeps stretching can wipe out many of them, which is why a stop loss is not optional.

Can you use both trend following and mean reversion?

Yes, as two separate setups that each have their own rules. The mistake is switching from one idea to the other in the middle of a trade to avoid taking a loss.
