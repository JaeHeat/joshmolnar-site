Should You Hold Trades Overnight?

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 6 min read

Should you hold trades overnight? Early in my trading career I did this more than I want to admit. I would have a winning trade at the close, convince myself the move had more to go, and hold it into the next morning. Sometimes it worked. More often, I woke up to a price that had jumped past my stop and handed me a loss bigger than anything I planned for. That one habit nearly ended my trading before it started.

What happens when you hold a trade overnight

When markets close for the day, price does not freeze. News breaks, economic data drops, and other markets around the world keep moving. When your market reopens, the first price can be very different from where it closed. That jump is called a gap, and the problem is simple. If price gaps past your stop loss, your stop does not fill at your planned price. It fills at whatever the market opens at, which can be far worse.

Think of it like locking your car in a parking lot and coming back the next morning. You set the alarm (your stop), but if someone tows the whole car (a gap), the alarm never gets a chance to go off. You just come back to an empty spot and a much bigger problem than you planned for.

Why overnight holds break your risk rules

The foundation of staying alive as a trader is controlling how much you lose on any single trade. I risk a small, fixed slice of my account every time, measured by the distance from my entry to my stop. That only works when the stop can actually fire at or near the price I set. Overnight gaps remove that protection. A trade you sized to lose 1 percent of your account can suddenly lose 3 or 4 percent, or worse, because the stop never had a chance to trigger at your level.

This is not a rare edge case. Gaps happen regularly around earnings, economic reports, and surprise geopolitical news. If you trade for a living, one blown risk event can undo weeks of good work.

Prop firm accounts make it even riskier

If you trade a funded prop firm account, the stakes are higher. Most prop firms have strict daily loss limits and maximum account loss limits. A single overnight gap that blows through your stop can breach those limits and cost you the account entirely. Many firms also have rules that require all positions closed before the session ends. Holding past the close is not just risky on these accounts. It can be an automatic rule violation that shuts you down on the spot.

Even firms that allow overnight holds often reduce your buying power or charge overnight fees. The math almost never works in your favor when you are trading someone else’s capital.

The real reason people hold overnight

Nobody holds overnight because the math supports it. They hold because a winning trade feels too good to close, or because a losing trade feels too painful to take. Both are emotional decisions dressed up as strategy. The winning hold is greed. The losing hold is denial. Neither belongs in a process that is supposed to be mechanical.

I have been there. The voice in your head says “this could run another 2 percent by morning.” What it does not say is that the expected overnight move could just as easily be against you, and your stop will not save you if it gaps. Recognizing that voice as emotion, not analysis, is one of the hardest parts of becoming a consistent trader who survives losing streaks.

When overnight holds might make sense

There is a difference between a day trader who impulsively holds a position and a swing trader who plans a multi-day trade from the start. Swing trading is a legitimate style, and if you design a trade to be held for days or weeks, you size it knowing that gaps are part of the deal. You use smaller position sizes, wider stops, and you accept that any single overnight event is already built into your plan.

The problem is when a day trade turns into a swing trade by accident. If you did not plan to hold overnight before you entered, you should not decide to hold overnight after the market moves. That decision should never be made in the middle of a trade.

My rule for overnight holds

My rule is simple. If I entered a trade as a day trade, I close it before the session ends. No exceptions. If I want to hold something overnight, it has to be planned that way from the start, with the right size, the right stop, and the understanding that a gap could take me out at a worse price than I planned. The decision is made before the trade, never during.

This sounds rigid, and it is. But rigidity is what keeps my daily loss limits intact and my funded accounts alive. The traders who last are not the ones who squeeze every last tick out of a move. They are the ones who protect the account first and let the profits take care of themselves over hundreds of trades.

The bottom line

Holding a day trade overnight is one of the fastest ways to take a loss bigger than you planned for. Gaps bypass your stop, break your risk rules, and can end a prop firm account in a single session. If you did not plan the hold before you entered, close the trade. The next opportunity is always tomorrow.

Common questions

Is it bad to hold day trades overnight?

For most day traders, yes. Overnight gaps can skip past your stop loss and cause a loss much larger than you planned. Unless the trade was designed as a swing trade from the start, closing before the session ends protects your risk rules.

What is gap risk in trading?

Gap risk is the chance that price jumps between the close and the next open, skipping past your stop loss. Your stop fills at the new market price, not the price you set, which can mean a much bigger loss than expected.

Can you hold trades overnight on a prop firm account?

Most prop firms require all positions closed before the session ends. Holding overnight can be an automatic rule violation. Even firms that allow it often reduce your buying power or charge fees, making the math worse.

What is the difference between holding overnight and swing trading?

Swing trading is planned from the start with smaller size and wider stops to account for overnight moves. Holding overnight is usually an unplanned decision made during a day trade, which means you are sized for intraday risk but exposed to multi-day risk.

Should I hold a winning trade overnight?

If it was entered as a day trade, no. The temptation to hold a winner overnight is greed, not strategy. Take the profit, protect your account, and plan a fresh trade the next session.
