Futures Trading Hours: When the Market Is Open

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

One of the first things that surprises people about futures is the clock. The stock market opens in the morning and closes in the afternoon. Futures barely close at all. Futures trading hours run almost around the clock, five days a week, and that sounds like freedom. I think it is closer to a trap if you do not understand it. Here is when the market is open, when it is shut, and which hours I actually trade.

What are futures trading hours?

I trade the big US stock index futures, so I will use those as the example. They open on Sunday at 6 p.m. Eastern time and they stay open until Friday at 5 p.m. Eastern. That is nearly five full days without a real close.

There is one short pause each day. From 5 p.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern, Monday through Thursday, the market shuts for one hour. Then it opens again and a new trading day begins.

So the simple picture looks like this.

  • Sunday 6 p.m. Eastern. The week opens.
  • Monday through Thursday, 5 p.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern. A one hour break.
  • Friday 5 p.m. Eastern. The week closes.
  • Saturday. Closed all day.

Other futures, like oil, gold and farm products, can keep slightly different hours. Holidays change the schedule too, and the times are not the same every year. The exchange posts the exact hours for every product on its website. Check there before you trade around a holiday instead of guessing.

Why the trading day starts at night

This part confuses almost everyone at first. In futures, a new trading day does not begin in the morning. It begins at 6 p.m. Eastern the evening before. So Monday’s trading day really starts on Sunday night, and Tuesday’s starts on Monday night.

It matters for one practical reason. If you trade a funded account, your daily loss limit often resets around that evening open, not at midnight and not when you wake up. A loss you take at 7 p.m. can count against tomorrow’s limit, not today’s. The exact reset time differs from firm to firm, so read your own rulebook. I cover those rules in my guide to prop firm trading, and it is worth knowing before a late trade costs you a day you have not even started.

Open does not mean worth trading

Here is the honest part. The market being open and the market being worth your time are two different things.

The US stock market runs from 9.30 in the morning to 4 in the afternoon Eastern. That is when the big players show up, and that is when futures on stock indexes are busiest. Outside those hours the market is still open, but far fewer people are trading. I think of it like a highway. At noon it is full of cars and it flows in a way you can read. At 3 a.m. it is nearly empty, and one truck changing lanes changes everything.

When fewer people are trading, three things tend to happen.

  • Price can sit still for hours, then jump with no warning.
  • The gap between the buying price and the selling price can get wider, so each trade costs you a little more.
  • Your stop can get filled at a worse price than you planned.

None of that makes the overnight hours impossible. It does make them a different game from the one most people practiced on. I go deeper on which windows move in the best time of day to trade.

The hours I actually trade

I trade a small window around the US stock market open. That is it. Most days I am finished before lunch. The market is open for roughly 23 hours a day and I use a couple of them.

That is not laziness. It is a rule. My setup was tested on those morning hours, so those are the only hours I trust it. The same setup at 2 a.m. is not the same trade, because the people on the other side are different and the market moves differently.

There is also a human reason. A market that never closes will happily take your money at midnight when you are tired, bored and trying to win back a bad morning. Late night trades taken to fix a bad day are one of the most common ways I see accounts get wrecked. A fixed window protects you from yourself.

How to pick your own trading hours

You do not need to copy my hours. You do need to choose yours on purpose. This is what I tell people I mentor.

  1. Pick one window of two or three hours that fits your real life and that you can show up for every day.
  2. Learn how the market behaves in that window, and only that window, before you add another.
  3. Write down a hard stop time. When the clock hits it, you are done, win or lose.
  4. Close your trades before the daily break unless holding through it is part of a written plan. I explain why in should you hold trades overnight.

If you have a day job, the long hours can help you. Someone on the West Coast can trade the open before work. Someone in Europe can trade it in the afternoon. Just be honest that the quiet hours are harder, not easier.

The bottom line

Futures trading hours run from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon with a one hour break each day. That is a fact about the market. It is not a schedule for you. Most traders lose money, and trading at every hour the screen is lit up is one of the quickest ways to join them. Pick a small window, learn it well and protect it. That kind of boring structure is a big part of what it takes to trade for a living.

Common questions

What time do futures open on Sunday?

US stock index futures open on Sunday at 6 p.m. Eastern time. That evening open is also the start of Monday's trading day.

Are futures open 24 hours a day?

Almost. US stock index futures trade about 23 hours a day from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon, with a one hour break from 5 p.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern on Monday through Thursday.

What time do futures close on Friday?

US stock index futures close for the week on Friday at 5 p.m. Eastern time and stay closed until Sunday at 6 p.m. Eastern.

Can you trade futures on the weekend?

Not on Saturday. US stock index futures are closed from Friday at 5 p.m. Eastern until they reopen on Sunday at 6 p.m. Eastern.

What is the best time to trade futures?

Stock index futures are busiest while the US stock market is open, from 9.30 in the morning to 4 in the afternoon Eastern. I trade a small window around that morning open and skip the quiet overnight hours.
