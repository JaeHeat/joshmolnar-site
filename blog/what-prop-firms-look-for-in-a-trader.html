What Prop Firms Actually Look for in a Trader

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

Most traders think the evaluation is about finding good entries. It is not. I have traded funded accounts for years, and the thing that separates traders who make it through the evaluation from those who do not is almost never the trading strategy. It is the behavior around the strategy. Here is what prop firms are actually looking for when they review your account.

Consistency is the number one signal

Prop firms are running a business. They need to trust that your results will be stable enough to plan around. A trader who makes three big wins in a row and then blows up on the fourth trade is not useful to them. What they want to see is a trader who makes the same size bet every time, follows their rules every day, and does not swing for the fences.

This is why many firms have a consistency rule that limits how much of your total gains can come from a single day. If your best day accounts for 40 percent of your profit, the firm gets nervous. That kind of result usually means you got lucky once, not that you have a repeatable process. The consistency rule explained goes deeper into how this is calculated and what it means for your trading.

How you handle losses matters more than you think

Anyone can look good in a winning stretch. What prop firms really want to know is what you do after a losing trade. Do you immediately enter a bigger position to get the money back? Do you start taking setups you normally would not? Or do you close the platform and come back tomorrow?

Revenge trading is one of the fastest ways to fail an evaluation. Firms can see the pattern in your trade log. A trader who keeps their bet size the same after a losing stretch is showing the kind of emotional control that makes them worth trusting with capital. A trader who doubles up after a loss is a liability.

Position sizing discipline

Consistent position sizing is one of the clearest signals in a trade log. If your size jumps around, it tells the firm something important. Either you do not have a process, or you let your emotions set your size. Neither is what they want to see.

I risk the same small percentage of the account on every single trade. It is not exciting. It is boring. But a boring, consistent trade log is exactly the kind of log that prop firms are built to look for before they hand you capital to manage.

Following the rules, all of them, all the time

This sounds obvious, but most evaluation failures happen because a trader broke a rule they knew existed. They forgot the news event restriction. They held a position past the cutoff. They did not check whether trading that asset was allowed.

Prop firms are not looking for traders who mostly follow the rules. They are looking for traders who follow them every single day. One rule violation at the wrong time can end an evaluation. Rule adherence is entirely in your control. It is a habits problem, not a skill problem.

Fewer trades, not more

New traders think more trades means more opportunity. Experienced traders know that every trade is a risk. Prop firms look favorably on traders who wait for their best setups and pass on everything else.

A trader who takes three high-quality trades a week shows restraint. A trader who takes thirty trades a week usually shows they are chasing or not being selective at all. Fewer, better trades is a pattern that stands out in a trade log.

Stopping when the day goes bad

One of the clearest green flags in a trade log is a trader who hits their daily loss limit and stops for the day. It tells the firm that you respect risk and that you will not let one bad session spiral. Setting a daily loss limit is the mechanical version of this. The behavioral version is actually stopping when you hit it, every time, without exception.

What prop firms are really grading

Prop firms are not grading your strategy. They are grading your process. Can you stay consistent when things are going well? Can you stay disciplined when things are going badly? Can you follow a simple set of rules every single day without exception?

Those are the questions your trade log answers. Build your process around answering them well. That is the foundation the whole thing is built on.

Common questions

What do prop firms look for in a trader?

They look for consistent position sizing, strict rule adherence, and controlled behavior after losses. A disciplined trade log matters more than a high win rate.

Do prop firms care about win rate?

Win rate matters less than consistency. A trader with a 40 percent win rate who follows every rule and sizes positions consistently is more attractive to a firm than a high win rate trader with wild sizing swings.

What gets traders cut from prop firms after getting funded?

Revenge trading after losses, inconsistent position sizing, and breaking the rules even once are the most common reasons funded traders lose their accounts.

What is the consistency rule at prop firms?

Most firms limit how much of your total profit can come from a single day, often around 30 to 50 percent. This filters out traders who made one lucky bet rather than showing a repeatable process.

How many trades per day do prop firms want to see?

There is no magic number, but fewer high-quality trades signal better discipline than a high number of trades. Most firms are looking for patience and selectivity, not volume.
