What Are Micro Futures? A Beginner's Guide

By Josh Molnar · September 2026 · 5 min read

I trade NQ futures every day. Micro NQ, specifically. That surprises a lot of people who assume you need a big account to touch the futures markets. Micro futures changed that, and if you are new to futures trading, they are probably the most useful thing to understand before you open a live account.

What are micro futures?

Micro futures are contracts that work exactly like standard futures contracts but are one-tenth the size. CME Group launched them in May 2019. The two most popular ones for day traders are the Micro E-mini S&P 500 (symbol MES) and the Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (symbol MNQ). If you want the full background on how futures contracts work in the first place, read what a futures contract actually is first. The short version: a futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date, and day traders use them to profit from intraday moves without ever owning the underlying asset.

How micro futures compare to standard contracts

The key difference is the dollar value of each move. Standard E-mini NQ moves twenty dollars per index point. At current Nasdaq levels, one NQ contract controls roughly 400,000 dollars of exposure. That is a large amount of leverage for a newer account trying to trade with proper risk rules.

Micro NQ moves two dollars per point. Exactly one-tenth. A ten-point move in the Nasdaq, which can happen in a slow hour, is worth 200 dollars on a standard NQ and 20 dollars on a micro NQ. For Micro E-mini S&P 500 (MES), one point is worth five dollars instead of fifty on the standard ES contract.

This matters because proper risk management means sizing your position so that a full loss never exceeds a small percentage of your account. With micro contracts, you can apply those same rules on a realistic account size instead of needing hundreds of thousands of dollars to size correctly.

Who micro futures are actually for

Micro futures are not a toy. They are a real instrument with real losses that happen in real time. What they do is make sensible position sizing possible without a large account.

I use micro contracts on funded prop accounts where the risk per trade needs to be precise and proportional to the account size. Many prop firms run their challenges on micro contracts for exactly this reason. If you are exploring the prop firm path, there is a good chance your challenge account will trade in micro territory.

They also work well as a learning instrument. If you are new to futures and want to practice live execution with real money at stake but without catastrophic exposure, starting on micro contracts is the sensible approach. The market reads are identical to standard contracts. The price charts, the levels, the sessions, the patterns are all the same. You are trading the same instrument at a smaller notional size.

Margin requirements and commission costs

Futures require margin, which is a deposit your broker holds while you have an open position. Think of it as a security deposit, not a purchase price. Micro contract intraday margins can be as low as 50 to 100 dollars per contract at some futures brokers. Overnight margin is higher, typically around 1,200 dollars per contract, because the exposure lasts longer.

Commissions are charged per contract, per side. On micro contracts that cost adds up if you are trading high frequency, so know your brokerage rates before you start.

A realistic starting account for micro futures trading is 2,000 to 5,000 dollars. Not because the minimum is that high, but because with less than that, even small per-point moves create pressure that leads to bad decisions. The path to trading for a living starts with not needing every single trade to work.

The mistake most beginners make

Because micro contracts are small, many people assume they can take more of them and treat each one as low stakes. That logic is backwards. If you trade ten micro NQ contracts at once because each one feels small, you are trading the equivalent of one standard NQ contract. The risk is exactly the same.

Trade one contract at a time when you are learning. The discipline of sizing correctly matters more than the size of the instrument. A trader who builds that habit on one micro contract is far ahead of someone trading ten and treating the position as disposable.

Why I trade micro contracts and not standard ones

I use micro NQ as my main trading instrument. Not because I cannot access the standard contract, but because sizing properly across multiple prop accounts requires the flexibility micro contracts give you. They let me apply the same risk-per-trade percentage across accounts of different sizes without being forced into awkward position sizing.

When I work with people I mentor, I always point them toward micro contracts first. Not as a stepping stone to move away from quickly, but because the skills you build on micro contracts are the exact same skills that work at any size. Risk management, entry discipline, reading a session, keeping a journal. None of that changes when the contract gets bigger. The habits do not change. Only the dollar values do.

Common questions

What are micro futures?

Micro futures are futures contracts that are one-tenth the size of standard E-mini contracts. CME Group launched them in 2019 to make futures trading accessible with smaller accounts and more precise position sizing.

What is the difference between micro and E-mini futures?

Size. Micro E-mini contracts are one-tenth as large as standard E-mini contracts. Micro NQ moves two dollars per index point; standard NQ moves twenty. The market, the price chart, and the sessions are identical.

How much money do you need to trade micro futures?

Intraday margins can be as low as 50 to 100 dollars per contract at some brokers. A realistic working account is 2,000 to 5,000 dollars so that a losing trade does not create desperate pressure to recover.

Are micro futures good for beginners?

Yes, for the right reason. They let you apply proper risk management on a smaller account size. The market reads are identical to standard contracts, so the skills transfer directly.

Can you trade micro futures on a prop firm challenge?

Many prop firms offer challenge accounts sized specifically for micro contracts. It is a common starting point for funded trading because the position sizing aligns with the account size and the risk rules.
