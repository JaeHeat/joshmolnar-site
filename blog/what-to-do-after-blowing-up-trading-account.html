What to Do After Blowing Up a Trading Account

By Josh Molnar · August 2026 · 7 min read

If you just blew up your trading account, I want to say something first: this is not the end. Most traders who stick around long enough will blow up at least one account, usually early in their career. I have talked to full-time traders who have done it more than once. What separates the ones who recover from the ones who quit or blow up a second account is not talent. It is what they do in the next few days.

Stop. Do not open another account yet

The worst thing you can do right now is immediately fund a new account. I know it feels like the solution. If you just get back in, you can win it back. That feeling is exactly what makes blow-ups so dangerous. When you are emotional after a loss of that size, your judgment is not reliable. I tell everyone I mentor the same thing: give yourself at least a week before you touch any live money again. Not because trading is bad. Because you are not in the right mental state to trade well right now.

The gap between a bad trading decision and a good one is often just time and distance from the loss.

Understand exactly what happened

This is the step most traders skip, which is why they repeat the same blow-up. Go through your trades and be brutally honest about the root cause. In my experience, most blow-ups come from one of three things:

  • Risking too much per trade. You were putting too large a slice of the account on each trade. When you hit a losing stretch, it did not take many losses to wipe everything out.
  • Revenge trading. You lost, you doubled up to get it back, you lost more, and the cycle continued until the account was gone.
  • A strategy that does not actually work. You traded something that never had a real edge and the randomness finally caught up with you.

The fix depends entirely on the cause. If you were sizing too big, the fix is simple in theory: risk less per trade. If it was revenge trading, that is a psychology problem and you need to work on that before you risk another dollar. If the strategy was flawed, you need to rebuild it from scratch using historical data before going live again.

Go back to the simulator

After you have figured out what went wrong, go back to paper trading or a trading simulator before risking real money again. I know this feels like a step backwards. It is not. It is the fastest way to rebuild both skill and confidence at the same time, with zero financial risk. If you cannot be consistently disciplined in a simulator, you will not be disciplined with live money on the line.

Two to four weeks of clean simulator trading with proper sizing is a reasonable benchmark before returning to live markets. Clean means no oversizing, no revenge setups, no breaking your own rules.

When you return to live trading, size down

When you fund a new account, whether with your own money or through a prop firm, start at the smallest position size you can trade. You want to feel the emotional reality of live money without putting the account at serious risk while your confidence and discipline are still rebuilding. The goal of your first month back is not profit. It is proving to yourself that you can follow your process under real conditions.

I cover exactly how to calculate the right position size based on your account and stop distance in my post on how much to risk per trade. Read that before you fund the next account.

The trap: the second account dies the same way

The second account is where most traders really end their trading career. They blow the first account, feel the sting, take a short break, open a new account with fresh motivation, and then make the exact same mistakes because they never fixed the root cause. The motivation fades fast when the losses start again.

Do not let the new account feel like a clean slate. Treat it as a continuation of the lesson you are supposed to be learning. Every rule you broke that cost you the first account needs to be written down and enforced before you put a dollar in the second one.

What real recovery looks like

Recovery from a blown account is not a week of good trades. It is months of consistently trading within your rules, building discipline, and slowly rebuilding your process. The traders who make it through this are not the ones who got lucky on a fast comeback. They are the ones who accepted that the blow-up was a tuition payment, took the lesson seriously, and rebuilt the right way.

If you want to understand the long-term math behind why surviving losing stretches is the foundation of every trading career, trading for a living starts with exactly that honest picture. Blow-ups happen. What you do with this one is what matters.

Common questions

How long should I stop trading after blowing up my account?

Give yourself at least a week before touching any live money. You need enough time and distance from the loss to make clear-headed decisions again, not emotional ones.

Should I open a new account right away after blowing up?

Not yet. Most traders who rush into a second account make the same mistakes and blow that one up too. Fix the root cause first, then return to a simulator before going live again.

Can you recover from blowing up a trading account?

Yes, but real recovery takes months of consistent, disciplined trading, not a quick winning streak. The traders who recover treat the blow-up as a lesson and rebuild their process before risking money again.

What causes most traders to blow up their accounts?

Usually one of three things: risking too much per trade, revenge trading after losses, or trading a strategy that never had a real edge to begin with.

Is a prop firm a good option after blowing up a personal account?

Possibly, but only after you have fixed the underlying problems. A prop firm is not a reset button. The same habits that blew up your account will cause problems in an evaluation too.
