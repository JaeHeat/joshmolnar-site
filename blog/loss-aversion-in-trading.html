Loss Aversion in Trading: Why Losses Hurt More

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

Here is a quick test. I offer you a coin flip. Heads, you win 100 dollars. Tails, you lose 100 dollars. Most people say no. The bet is perfectly fair, and it still feels like a bad deal. That feeling has a name. It is called loss aversion, and loss aversion in trading is behind more bad decisions than any chart pattern or bad signal I have ever seen. If you have ever stared at a losing trade and refused to close it, you have already met it.

What loss aversion in trading actually means

Loss aversion is a simple idea. Losing something hurts more than gaining the same thing feels good. Two researchers, Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, wrote about it in 1979, and Kahneman later won a Nobel Prize for the work. The rough rule that came out of their research is that a loss stings about twice as hard as an equal win feels good.

The exact number is still argued about, and it changes from person to person. I do not need the exact number. I only need you to notice the feeling. Losing 100 dollars does not feel like the opposite of winning 100 dollars. It feels worse. Your brain is not keeping a fair score.

How it shows up on your screen

That unfair scorekeeping pushes traders into the same three habits again and again.

  • Holding losers too long. A loss on the screen is not real yet. Closing the trade makes it real, and real hurts. So you wait and hope. I wrote a full post on why you hold losing trades too long.
  • Cutting winners too early. A small win on the screen feels like something you could lose. So you grab it fast, long before your plan said to.
  • Moving the stop. Price gets close to your exit, and you slide the exit a little further away. Then a little further. The small planned loss quietly turns into a big unplanned one.

This is not just a beginner thing. A well known study looked at 10,000 real brokerage accounts and found the same pattern. People were much quicker to sell their winners than their losers.

Why this quietly wrecks good strategies

Picture a trader with a solid plan. The plan says to risk 100 dollars to make 200 dollars. On paper that works fine, even if the trader loses more often than they win.

Now add loss aversion. The trader grabs wins early at 80 dollars because it feels safe. The trader lets losses run to 180 dollars because closing hurts. Same entries. Same charts. But the wins are now smaller than the losses, and a plan that worked on paper slowly bleeds in real life.

That is the part I want you to sit with. Most traders who lose do not lose because their idea was terrible. They lose because the feeling of a loss changes what they do after they click the button. It is one of the big reasons I am honest that most people who try to trade for a living do not make it.

How I keep it from running my trades

You cannot switch this feeling off. I still feel it after years of doing this. What you can do is make your decisions before the feeling shows up.

  • Decide the exit before the entry. I know where I am wrong and where I take profit before I place the trade. The calm version of me makes the plan. The stressed version only has to follow it.
  • Risk an amount that is boring. If a loss makes your stomach drop, the trade is too big. I keep it small and fixed, which I explain in how much to risk per trade.
  • Let the orders do the work. My stop and my target go in as real orders. I am not sitting there deciding in the moment whether to take the loss.
  • Count in groups, not single trades. One loss tells you almost nothing. I judge myself on the last 20 trades, so no single red trade feels like the end of the world.
  • Write down the urge. Every time I want to move a stop or grab an early win, I note it in my journal. Seeing how often the urge was wrong takes away some of its power.

Why it bites harder on a funded account

On a prop firm account, loss aversion gets a second engine. You are not only afraid of losing money. You are afraid of losing the account itself. That fear makes people trade tiny and scared when they are near the limit, or swing big to escape it. Both are the same feeling wearing different clothes. The fix is the same too. Fixed small risk, exits decided ahead of time, and no changes in the middle of a trade.

The bottom line

Loss aversion is not a flaw in you. It is how human brains are built. Losses feel bigger than wins, and that tilt nudges you to hold losers, cut winners, and move stops. You will not out-think it in the moment. You beat it by deciding early, risking small, and letting your rules make the hard call for you. None of this makes trading safe, and it does not make anyone a winner. It just removes one of the most common ways traders hurt themselves.

Common questions

What is loss aversion in trading?

It is the habit of feeling a loss more strongly than a win of the same size. In trading it pushes people to hold losing trades, close winning trades early, and move their stops.

Why do losses hurt more than gains feel good?

Human brains do not keep a fair score. Research by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky found a loss stings roughly twice as hard as an equal win feels good, though the exact amount varies by person.

How do I overcome loss aversion as a trader?

You cannot remove the feeling, so decide before it shows up. Set your exit before you enter, risk a small fixed amount, and place your stop and target as real orders so you are not deciding in the moment.

Is loss aversion the same as holding losing trades too long?

Holding losers is one result of it, not the whole thing. Loss aversion also causes traders to take profits too early and to move their stops further away.
