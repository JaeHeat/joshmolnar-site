How to Draw Trend Lines the Right Way (Simple Rules)

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

Open any chart online and you will see lines all over it. Most of them are trend lines, and most of them are drawn badly. Learning how to draw trend lines is one of the first chart skills a new trader picks up, and it is also one of the easiest to fool yourself with. I trade for a living, and I draw far fewer than I used to. Here are the simple rules I follow, the mistakes I see most, and what a trend line can and cannot do for you.

What a trend line actually is

A trend line is a straight line that shows which way price is leaning. That is all it is.

Picture a ball bouncing up a staircase. Each bounce lands a little higher than the last one. If you laid a ruler along the spots where the ball landed, the ruler would tilt upward. That ruler is a trend line. It just shows the path the ball has been taking.

When price keeps making higher low points, the market is leaning up. When it keeps making lower high points, it is leaning down. I explain those steps in more detail in my post on market structure. A trend line is simply a clean way to see them.

How to draw trend lines in four steps

  1. Zoom out first. Look at the big picture before you draw anything. A line that only fits the last twenty minutes is usually just noise.
  2. Find the turning points. In a rising market, mark the low points where price dipped and then turned back up. In a falling market, mark the high points where price popped and then turned back down.
  3. Connect two of them. For a rising market, draw the line under the lows. For a falling market, draw the line over the highs. Under for up, over for down.
  4. Wait for a third touch. Any two points on a chart can be joined by a line, so two touches prove nothing. When price comes back a third time and turns at the line, now you have something worth watching.

Then stretch the line out to the right, into the empty part of the chart. That is where it becomes useful, because it shows you where price might react next.

The mistakes that make a trend line useless

Most bad trend lines come from the same few habits.

  • Forcing the line to fit. If you have to tilt it, nudge it, and ignore half the candles to make it work, the line is not there. A real one is obvious. You should not have to argue with the chart.
  • Drawing it on the wrong side. New traders often draw a line over the highs in a rising market. That shows you the top of the move, which is a different tool. The trend line sits under a rising market and over a falling one.
  • Trusting a very steep line. A line that shoots almost straight up will break quickly, because price cannot climb that fast for long. A gentle slope tends to last longer.
  • Drawing too many. Ten lines on a chart will always give you one that looks right afterward. That is not skill. I keep one or two and delete the rest.
  • Moving the line after it breaks. If price goes through your line and you redraw it to keep your idea alive, you are no longer reading the chart. You are protecting your opinion.

One honest note. Traders argue about whether to draw through the candle bodies or the thin wicks. I do not think it matters much. Pick one way and do it the same way every time. A line is a zone, not a laser. Price can poke through it a little and still respect it.

What a trend line can and cannot tell you

A trend line is a ruler, not a crystal ball. It tells you where price has turned before. It does not promise price will turn there again.

I use it the same way I use support and resistance. It marks a place where I pay attention. If price pulls back to a rising line and I see my setup there, I have a clear spot for my stop on the other side. If price cuts through the line and stays there, that tells me the lean may be changing, and I stop looking for trades in the old direction.

What I never do is buy or sell just because price touched a line. A line by itself is not a reason to risk money. Lines break all the time, and nobody can know ahead of time which touch will be the one that fails. That is why every trade I take has a stop and a small fixed amount at risk, which is the core of the process I lay out in my guide to trading for a living.

How to practice drawing trend lines

Pick one market and one chart. Scroll back in time so you cannot see what happened next. Draw your line using the four steps, then move forward one candle at a time and watch what price does when it comes back.

Do that fifty times and write down what you see. You will learn two things. Some lines hold nicely. Plenty do not. Seeing both with your own eyes teaches you to treat the line as a guide and not a guarantee.

The bottom line

Zoom out, connect the clear turning points, wait for a third touch, and never force it. Most traders lose money, and a line on a chart will not change that by itself. What a good trend line can do is give you a simple, honest picture of which way the market is leaning, so your plan and your risk rules have something clear to work with.

Common questions

How many points do you need to draw a trend line?

You need two turning points to draw the line and a third touch to trust it. Any two points can be joined, so the third touch is what shows the line may matter.

Do you draw trend lines on wicks or candle bodies?

Either can work. Pick one way and use it every time, and treat the line as a zone that price can poke through a little.

Where do you draw a trend line in an uptrend?

Under the rising low points. In a falling market you draw it over the falling high points.

What does it mean when a trend line breaks?

It means the market may be changing the way it leans. It is a signal to stop looking for trades in the old direction, not a promise of a big move the other way.

Are trend lines reliable?

They are a guide, not a guarantee. Some hold and plenty break, so a trend line should mark a place to pay attention and never be the only reason to risk money.
