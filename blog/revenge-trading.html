What Is Revenge Trading? How to Stop

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 6 min read

Revenge trading is one of those things almost every trader has done at least once. You take a loss, you feel frustrated, and instead of walking away you immediately jump back in to win it back. The next trade is not about your setup or your plan. It is about the loss you just took. That is revenge trading, and it is one of the fastest ways to turn a bad day into a catastrophic one.

What is revenge trading?

Revenge trading is when you take a trade purely because you want to recover a recent loss. It is not planned. It does not follow your rules. The only reason you are clicking the button is because you are angry, frustrated, or embarrassed about the loss that just happened. The trade feels urgent, like you need to fix something right now. That urgency is the tell.

The problem is that this next trade almost never fixes anything. You are trading from emotion, not from your trading edge. And when you trade without an edge, you are just gambling with extra steps.

Why revenge trading destroys accounts

A single loss, even a painful one, rarely ends a trading career. What ends careers is the chain reaction after that loss. Here is how it usually plays out.

  • You take a normal loss. It stings but it is within your plan.
  • Instead of stepping away, you take another trade immediately. This one is bigger, or sloppier, or both.
  • That trade loses too. Now you are down twice as much and twice as angry.
  • You take a third trade, even bigger. By now your rules are completely out the window.

One loss becomes three, four, five. Your daily loss doubles or triples in minutes. If you trade prop firm accounts, this is exactly how people breach their daily loss limit and lose the account in a single session. The original loss was survivable. The revenge spiral was not.

How to recognize it in yourself

Revenge trading is easy to spot in hindsight but hard to catch in real time. That is what makes it dangerous. Here are the warning signs to watch for.

  • Speed. You enter the next trade within seconds or minutes of the last loss, without any real analysis.
  • Size. You increase your position size to make back the loss faster.
  • Anger. You feel a physical tension, a clenched jaw, a tight chest, a need to prove something.
  • No setup. If someone asked you to explain the trade, you could not point to a rule in your plan that triggered it.

If even one of those is true, you are probably revenge trading. Two or more and there is no question.

How to stop revenge trading

The fix is not willpower. Willpower fails when emotions are running hot. You need systems that make revenge trading physically harder to do.

Set a daily loss limit and enforce it. Decide before the day starts that if you lose a certain amount, you are done. Not “maybe done.” Done. Close the platform. I use a hard daily cap on every account I trade, and when I hit it, the screen goes off. No exceptions. I wrote about this in detail in my post on daily loss limits.

Use a cooldown timer. After any loss, wait a fixed amount of time before your next trade. Even five or ten minutes is enough to let the emotional spike pass. The best trades do not require speed. If the opportunity is real, it will still be there after a short break.

Write before you trade. Before entering the next trade after a loss, write down the setup in one sentence. If you cannot describe it clearly, you do not have a trade. You have an impulse. A trading for a living mindset means treating every entry like a business decision, not an emotional reaction.

Reduce size after a loss. Some traders cut their position size in half after a losing trade. This does two things. It limits the damage if the revenge impulse sneaks through. And it forces you to accept smaller potential gains, which removes the “win it all back” fantasy that drives the behavior.

The real lesson

Revenge trading is not a strategy problem. It is a self-awareness problem. The market does not know or care that you just lost money. It is not punishing you, and it is not going to reward you for trying harder. Every trade is independent. The previous result has zero influence on what happens next.

The traders who survive long enough to become profitable are not the ones who never feel the urge. They are the ones who built rules that protect them from acting on it. That is the difference between knowing something is bad for you and actually having a system that prevents it.

If you are honest with yourself and you know this is a pattern, fix the system, not the feeling. The feeling is human. The system is what keeps you in the game.

Common questions

What is revenge trading?

Revenge trading is when you take a trade purely to recover a recent loss, without following your plan or rules. It is driven by emotion, not by a real setup.

Why is revenge trading so dangerous?

Because one unplanned trade usually leads to several more, each bigger and sloppier than the last. A single manageable loss can spiral into a blown account in minutes.

How do I stop revenge trading?

Set a hard daily loss limit, use a cooldown timer after every loss, and require yourself to write down the setup before entering the next trade. Systems beat willpower.

Is revenge trading the same as overtrading?

They overlap but they are not identical. Overtrading means taking too many trades in general. Revenge trading is specifically triggered by a recent loss and the urge to win it back.
