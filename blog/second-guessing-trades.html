How to Stop Second Guessing Your Trades

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

If you have ever entered a trade, immediately wondered if you should get out, and then watched it go exactly where you planned without you on it, you already know what second guessing costs. It is not caution. It is a habit. And if you do not fix it, it will quietly eat your edge no matter how good your strategy is. Here is how to stop second guessing your trades, from someone who had to break this loop himself.

Why you second guess trades in the first place

Your brain is built to avoid pain. A losing trade is pain. So after a few losses your brain starts looking for reasons to skip, exit early, or hesitate on the next setup. The problem is that your brain does not care whether the trade was a good process trade or a bad one. It just remembers the sting. So it starts vetoing perfectly valid setups as a way to protect you from feeling that sting again.

This is the core loop. You take a loss, your brain flags the next similar setup as dangerous, you hesitate or skip, the trade works without you, and now you feel even worse. The next time you force yourself in, you are tight and anxious and you bail at the first wiggle. Rinse and repeat until you are either paralyzed or revenge trading to make up for all the ones you skipped.

Step one. Separate the decision from the outcome

Most traders judge a trade by whether it made money. That is the wrong scoreboard. A good trade is one that followed your rules. A bad trade is one that did not. Some good trades lose. Some bad trades win. If you grade yourself on outcome, every loss trains your brain to second guess the next entry, even when the entry was correct.

I tell people I mentor to write one word next to every trade in their trading journal. “Process” or “No process.” That single word matters more than the dollar figure. Over weeks it rewires how you think about each trade, because you start seeing that process trades cluster into profit even when individual ones lose.

Step two. Make the trade decision before the market opens

Second guessing thrives in real time. When you are staring at a moving candle your brain has a thousand reasons to hesitate. The fix is to move the decision earlier. Before the session opens, write down the exact setups you are looking for, the exact entry rules, and the exact stop and target. When the setup shows up, you are not deciding. You are executing a plan you already committed to.

This is why a trading checklist is not just an organizational tool. It is a psychological tool. The checklist makes the trade feel like following directions instead of making a judgment call under pressure. Following directions is easy. Making real-time judgment calls when money is on the line is where second guessing lives.

Step three. Shrink the risk until hesitation disappears

If you are second guessing entries, your size might be too big. Not too big for your account, too big for your current confidence. There is no shame in cutting your position size in half until you can execute without flinching. The goal is reps, not dollars. Once you are clicking the button without hesitation on small size, you slowly build back up. I have seen traders completely fix their second guessing problem just by trading smaller for two or three weeks.

This is the same logic behind how I think about trading for a living. You do not get good and then go full size. You go small, get clean reps, build trust in the process, and then size up when execution is automatic.

Step four. Stop watching every tick after you enter

Once the trade is on, set your stop and your target, and step away from the screen. Watching every tick gives your brain raw material to manufacture doubt. Every pullback becomes a reason to exit early. Every stall becomes proof you were wrong. But if your stop and target are set, there is nothing to do. The trade will either hit your stop or your target. Watching it happen in real time does not change the outcome. It only changes how you feel, and feelings are what created the problem in the first place.

If you cannot fully step away, at least shrink the chart to a higher timeframe so you are not reacting to noise. The traders I know who struggle the most with second guessing are almost always the ones glued to one-minute candles.

Step five. Track the trades you skipped

This is the one that changed everything for me. Every time I skipped a valid setup because of hesitation, I wrote it down and tracked what it would have done. After a few weeks I had a clear record showing that the trades I skipped performed the same as the trades I took. My brain was not protecting me. It was costing me. Seeing that in your own data is more powerful than any advice.

If your skipped trades actually do worse, then your hesitation might be worth exploring. But in my experience the skips are random. The fear is not useful information. It is just noise from the last loss.

The real fix is trusting your process

Second guessing is a trust problem. You do not trust your strategy, your rules, or yourself. The only way to build that trust is to have a clear trading plan, follow it mechanically, and review the results honestly. Over hundreds of trades the evidence stacks up and the doubt fades, not because you become fearless, but because you have seen the proof that your process works.

If you do not have a plan worth trusting yet, that is the real problem to solve. Second guessing is a symptom. The plan is the cure.

Common questions

Why do I keep second guessing my trades?

Your brain remembers the pain of past losses and tries to protect you by creating doubt before the next entry. It is a survival instinct, not a trading instinct, and it fires whether the setup is good or bad.

Is second guessing trades a sign I should change my strategy?

Not necessarily. If your strategy has a tested edge and you are following the rules, the second guessing is usually about confidence, not about the strategy itself. Track your skipped trades to see whether your hesitation is adding value or just costing you.

How do I build confidence in my trading decisions?

Trade smaller so execution feels easy, follow a written checklist so each trade is a process rather than a judgment call, and review your journal weekly to see that process trades cluster into profit over time.

Should I use a checklist to reduce second guessing?

Yes. A checklist moves the decision from real time, where pressure and emotion live, to your pre-session planning, where you can think clearly. If the setup checks every box, you execute. No debate needed.

How long does it take to stop second guessing trades?

Most traders see a clear improvement within two to four weeks of consistent checklist use and journaling. The doubt does not vanish overnight, but it fades as your track record of followed rules builds up.
