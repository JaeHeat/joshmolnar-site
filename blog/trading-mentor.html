Do You Actually Need a Trading Mentor?

By Josh Molnar · August 2026 · 5 min read

People ask me this a lot, usually after they have already spent money on something that did not work. So I will give you the honest version, from someone who both mentors traders and was in your seat a few years ago wondering the same thing.

The short answer is that it depends. And the more important question is what kind of mentor you are actually looking for.

What a real trading mentor actually does

A mentor who is worth anything is not handing you a strategy and walking away. They are looking at your actual trades, asking why you took them, finding the specific thing in your process that keeps costing you money, and shortening the time it takes to fix it. Think of it this way. You could figure out everything on your own, but you would pay the market to teach you every lesson. A real mentor compresses that. They have already made the mistakes you are about to make.

What I do with the people I mentor is simple. We look at real trades, real charts, real decisions. Not theories. The value is never in the strategy itself. The value is in someone who has already been where you are seeing exactly where your process is breaking down, It almost always breaks down in one of three places. You are sizing too large, breaking your own rules under pressure, or chasing setups that are not quite there yet.

When mentorship actually helps

If you are newer to trading, the main problem is that you do not know what you do not know. You can read every book, watch every video, and still not realize you are placing stops based on what you can afford to lose rather than where the trade is actually wrong. A good mentor catches that kind of thing in ten minutes. Left alone, it might take you a year to figure out.

The traders I have seen benefit most from mentorship are not complete beginners. They are people who have some real experience but feel stuck. They are not blowing up. They are just not improving. Usually one or two specific things are holding them back, and they need someone outside their own head to find those things.

When you probably do not need one

If you have not done the basics yet, a mentor will not help you. The basics are a written trading plan, a journal with every trade logged, and at least several weeks of paper trading to test your process. If those three things are not in place, no one can tell you what is wrong because you do not have enough data yet. Start there first.

If what you are really looking for is someone to tell you what to trade, that is not a mentor. That is a signal service, and signal services teach you nothing. What you need is someone who makes you better at making your own decisions, not someone who makes the decisions for you.

How to spot a real mentor from a guru

This is where most people get burned. The trading education industry is full of people who made their money selling courses, not trading. Here is how to tell the difference.

  • A real mentor shows you their own track record, including the losing periods. Consistent wins with no visible losses is a red flag, not a credential.
  • A real mentor focuses on your process, not your outcomes. If the pitch is about how much money you will make, walk away.
  • A real mentor reviews your actual trades. If it is all video lectures and no personal feedback on your real work, that is a course, not mentorship.
  • A real mentor is honest that most traders lose, even people they work with, because the problem is almost always execution and psychology, not strategy.

The honest bottom line

The right mentor, someone who actually trades, reviews your work personally, and has been through the same failures you are going through, can compress your timeline and help you fix problems you did not even know you had. The wrong mentor is just expensive.

The question is not really whether to get a mentor. The question is whether the specific person you are considering actually trades every day, actually looks at your work, and is honest with you when your process is broken. Most are not. The ones who are, are worth it.

Common questions

Do you need a trading mentor to become profitable?

No, but the right mentor can cut years off your learning curve. The key is finding someone who actually trades and reviews your specific work, not someone selling a generic course.

How much does a trading mentor cost?

It varies widely. Group programs often start around a few hundred dollars a month; private coaching can run into thousands. Price alone tells you nothing about whether the mentor actually trades or gives real feedback.

How do you find a legitimate trading mentor?

Look for someone who shows their actual track record including losses, gives personal feedback on your trades, and is honest about the reality that most traders lose. Anyone who promises results is a red flag.

What is the difference between a trading mentor and a trading course?

A course delivers content and you apply it alone. A real mentor reviews your actual trades and tells you specifically what you are doing wrong. The feedback loop is what makes the difference.

When should you look for a trading mentor?

After you have the basics in place: a written plan, a trade journal, and real paper trading experience. If you do not have those yet, start there first.
