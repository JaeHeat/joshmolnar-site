How to Trade Around News Events: What I Actually Do

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

When I first started day trading futures, news events cost me money on a regular basis. Not because my setups were bad. Because good setups turned into fast, violent moves the moment a report dropped and my stop was already on the wrong side of the range. The lesson took a while to really absorb: figuring out how to trade around news events is not a strategy problem. It is a risk management decision, and it is one you make before the market opens.

What counts as a high-impact news event

There are hundreds of economic reports released every month. Most of them move price by noise. A handful move it enough that everything else stops mattering for a few minutes. The four I watch closely are FOMC rate decisions and meeting minutes (the Federal Reserve meets eight times a year), the monthly jobs report, the Consumer Price Index, and quarterly GDP releases. These are the reports that cause dramatic, fast moves in equities and futures. Everything else is background noise for most day trading setups.

The economic calendar is the tool you check before every session. The goal is not to predict the number. The goal is to know where the landmines are before you walk into the field.

Why news events are dangerous for day traders specifically

The problem is not volatility in general. Volatility is what day trading runs on. The problem is what a news spike does to the conditions your setup relies on. A price action setup works because if support holds, buyers will step in. During a major release, most buyers and sellers are standing flat waiting for the number. The moment it drops, liquidity thins out and price moves fast, often through your stop, then back and through it again. What feels like a stop-hunt is usually just the market repricing faster than any retail trader can react. Your setup did not fail. The conditions it needed temporarily ceased to exist.

The three ways traders handle news events

There are really only three approaches, and I have tried all of them:

  • Stay flat before the release. Exit any open position or avoid new entries for a window around the report. For FOMC I go flat at least thirty minutes before the announcement. For monthly jobs and inflation data, fifteen to twenty minutes is usually enough. You give up some potential profit. You eliminate the possibility of a fast loss that undoes multiple days of work.
  • Reduce position size. Some traders keep partial exposure but cut size significantly before the release. This is reasonable if a trade has already moved well in your favor and you want some continued exposure without full risk on the table.
  • Trade through it at normal size. This is what most beginners do by default, and it is how you occasionally get a loss that ends your day in one candle. There is no edge in holding a full-sized position through a report you cannot predict. The directional move is random relative to your setup.

I use the first option for high-impact events. It is the least exciting choice and the most consistent one over time.

Why this matters even more on a funded account

If you trade funded prop firm accounts, news risk is not just about your own money. Prop firms set a maximum daily loss. A single news spike can hit that limit in one candle and end your trading day. In the worst case it triggers a rules breach you cannot walk back. The same discipline that protects your personal capital becomes a formal requirement on a funded account. The good news is that the right behavior is identical either way.

Using the economic calendar before every session

The simplest habit you can build is checking the economic calendar before you sit down to trade. I do this every morning. You learn what is on the schedule, how big the expected move is, and when to plan your flat window. It takes less than a minute and it removes the scenario where you get caught in a news spike you forgot was coming. Most trading platforms include a built-in calendar. If yours does not, a free one takes thirty seconds to find.

The bigger point about consistency

Traders who build long, consistent track records are not better at predicting news outcomes. They are more careful about which conditions they accept risk in. News events are one of the clearest cases in day trading where the conditions are simply not in your favor as a retail trader. Stepping aside is not a missed opportunity. It is evidence that you understand risk in a way most people do not. What I tell people I mentor who want to trade for a living is this: protecting your edge matters as much as having one. That means knowing when to sit out.

Common questions

Should I trade during FOMC?

For most retail day traders, the safer choice is to be flat or in minimal size before FOMC announcements. The move is fast, often random relative to your setup, and can blow through normal stop levels in seconds.

How long before news should I be out of trades?

For high-impact events like FOMC, I exit at least thirty minutes early. For monthly inflation and jobs reports, fifteen to twenty minutes before the release is usually enough.

Can you profit from trading news events as a day trader?

Some traders specialize in news plays, but it requires a very different process than regular day trading. For most people building a consistent edge, sitting out major events and protecting existing setups is more valuable than chasing news-driven moves.

How do I know which news events are high-impact?

An economic calendar marks events by impact level. The four to know by heart are FOMC, NFP (the monthly jobs report), CPI (inflation), and GDP. These drive most of the big fast moves in equity futures.

Does this apply to crypto trading as well?

Yes. Crypto reacts to the same macro events, though markets run around the clock and liquidity gaps can be less extreme. The same principle applies: check the calendar before every session and decide in advance how you will handle big data days.
